## 3. Dimension B: institutional economy

Dimension B asks which households held the economy and produced its records. It reflects the long debate between the temple-city model (Deimel, Falkenstein), its critique (Gelb, Foster), Diakonoff's two-sector model and later views of the patrimonial household (Schloen). PAL, TEMP and PRIV measure weight in the written record, not only in the economy, so they are sensitive to which archives happen to be excavated. PRIV enters index B reversed (3 minus PRIV), because a large private sector means less institutional control of the economy.

### PAL: Weight of the palace (crown) sector

**Definition.** The weight of the palace (crown household) in the economy and in the surviving record.

**Why it matters.** If the palace is the dominant producer of documents, a single household's practice sets the norm for a large share of tablets.

**Origin.** No Seshat equivalent; Gelb 1979; Diakonoff 1982; Renger 1994; Schloen 2001.

| Score | Anchor | Operational test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | marginal | Marginal or absent from the record. |
| 1 | present, minor in the record | Present but minor in the record. |
| 2 | a major institution | A major institution. |
| 3 | dominant institution of the economy and of the written record | The dominant institution of the economy and the written record. |

**Records that show it.** share of archives from palace or crown households.

**Decision rules and boundary cases.**

- Score weight in the record, and note when it may reflect excavation rather than history.
- Old Assyrian scores 0 because the texts never mention a palace (Veenhof 2008 p. 39); this is evidence of absence from the record, not silence.
- Ebla scores 3: the archive is a palace archive and the palace runs the economy.

**Across periods.** Ebla (3) and Old Assyrian (0) are the two extremes: the Ebla archive is the palace's own, while the Old Assyrian record never mentions a palace.

### TEMP: Weight of the temple sector

**Definition.** The weight of temple households in the economy and in the surviving record.

**Why it matters.** Temple archives are among the most uniform corpora we measure (Girsu, Eanna, Hellenistic Uruk). TEMP lets us test whether institution type, rather than state power, predicts uniformity.

**Origin.** No Seshat equivalent; the temple-city debate (Deimel, Falkenstein, Gelb, Foster); Jursa 2010 and Kleber 2008 for the first millennium.

| Score | Anchor | Operational test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | marginal | Marginal. |
| 1 | present, minor | Present but minor. |
| 2 | major institution | A major institution. |
| 3 | dominant institution of economy and record | The dominant institution of the economy and the record. |

**Records that show it.** share of archives from temple households.

**Decision rules and boundary cases.**

- ED IIIb is contested (2-3): Steinkeller 1993 p. 122 describes self-sufficient temple households controlling most resources, while Yoffee 1995 pp. 289-290 and Schrakamp (as cited by Bartash 2020) argue that temples were subordinate to the palace.
- Ebla scores 0: Steinkeller 1993 p. 124 says the temple household of southern type did not exist there.
- Hellenistic scores 3 on Clancier 2011 p. 757 (temple herds and fields) and Monerie and Clancier 2023 p. 73. The surviving cuneiform record of the period comes mainly from the temples of Uruk and Babylon, so this score depends strongly on what was recovered.

**Across periods.** Neo-Assyrian (1) and Neo-Babylonian (3) mark the north-south contrast of the first millennium.

### TCTL: Crown control of temples

**Definition.** The degree of crown control over temples: from autonomous temples to temples run as units of the state.

**Why it matters.** If the crown controls temples, temple archives become channels of state practice; if not, they are independent producers.

**Origin.** No Seshat equivalent; Jursa 2010; Kleber 2008; Sallaberger and Westenholz 1999.

| Score | Anchor | Operational test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | autonomous | Temples autonomous. |
| 1 | royal patronage only | Royal patronage only (gifts, building). |
| 2 | royal appointees, levies on temples | Royal appointees in temples, or levies on temples. |
| 3 | temples run as units of the state | Temples run as units of the state, with royal officials auditing their accounts. |

**Records that show it.** royal officials (qīpu, ša rēš šarri, šabra appointed by king), temple accounts audited by the crown.

**Decision rules and boundary cases.**

- Look for royal officials inside temples (šabra appointed by the king, qīpu, ša rēš šarri) and royal audits.
- Ur III is contested (2-3): Steinkeller 1987 p. 17 and Garfinkle 2022 pp. 143, 149 describe temples as state property under royal governors, while Sallaberger 1999 pp. 287-288 places control with the provincial governor (ensi2) rather than the crown.
- Middle Babylonian is contested (1-3): Sassmannshausen 2001 p. 181 sees royal kin as chancellors of Nippur with levies from much of Babylonia, while Brinkman 2004 p. 287 finds the evidence too scattered for such conclusions.

**Across periods.** ED IIIb scores 3 on Charpin 1995 p. 809 and Bartash 2020 (rulers of Umma and Lagaš had been temple administrators); Yoffee 1995 p. 289 implies 2. ED IIIa scores 1 on a single qualifying statement (the House of the Lass was close to the ruler).

### PRIV: Private (house) sector in writing [reversed in indices]

**Definition.** The weight of private (family) archives in the written record.

**Why it matters.** Private archives are produced by many independent writers; a record dominated by them should be less uniform. PRIV enters index B reversed.

**Origin.** No Seshat equivalent; Diakonoff's two-sector model; Van De Mieroop 1992; Garfinkle 2012; Schloen 2001.

| Score | Anchor | Operational test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | negligible | Negligible. |
| 1 | some private legal documents | Some private legal documents (sales, loans). |
| 2 | substantial private archives | Substantial private archives. |
| 3 | private archives dominate the written record | Private archives dominate the written record. |

**Records that show it.** share of private/family archives (Archibab, Prosobab, CDLI archive field).

**Decision rules and boundary cases.**

- Score the share of the record, not the size of the private economy.
- A 0/1 split between sources is rounded down to 0 (ED IIIa, Ebla); the reader may prefer 1.
- A silent record (no private documents survive) is scored 0 by the current rule; Hittite and Middle Elamite are candidates for 'insufficient evidence'.

**Across periods.** Early and full Old Babylonian (3) and Old Assyrian (3) are the periods of private archives; Ur III is contested (1-2): Garfinkle 2010 p. 186 and 2022 p. 163 stress the autonomy of merchants against the institutional picture.
