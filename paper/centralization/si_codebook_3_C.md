## 4. Dimension C: control of writing and norms

Dimension C asks how far the state shaped writing practice and the norms written documents follow. These parameters are closest to our outcome, the form of tablets. To keep the outcome out of its own predictor they should rest on statements about procedure, training, law and standards. Some quoted statements also mention tablet shape or format; they are marked [format] in the tables, and Section 5 shows what happens to the scores without them.

### BUR: Standardized administrative procedure

**Definition.** Standardized administrative procedure: from no detectable conventions to the same procedures applied across provinces.

**Why it matters.** Standard procedure means standard document types, which the bureaucratic hypothesis expects to produce standard tablets.

**Origin.** Seshat *Full-time bureaucrats* (partial); Weber 1922; Postgate 2013; Steinkeller 2003; the debate on the Ur III bureaucratic state.

| Score | Anchor | Operational test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | none detectable | No standard procedure detectable. |
| 1 | local conventions | Local conventions only. |
| 2 | standardized accounting within major institutions | Standardized accounting within the major institutions. |
| 3 | standardized procedures across provinces (balanced accounts, instructions for officials, audits) | Standardized procedures across provinces: balanced accounts, instructions to officials, audits. |

**Records that show it.** balanced accounts, instructions, standardized document types.

**Decision rules and boundary cases.**

- Score from statements about procedure (account types, audits, instructions), not from tablet form.
- Old Akkadian is contested (2-3): Charpin 1995 p. 810 and Yoffee 1995 p. 293 describe standardized accounting under royal control, while Schrakamp 2020 (PDF p. 565) and Foster 1982 p. 22 point to local dating, measures and accounting.
- Hellenistic scores 1: royal administration wrote on leather in alphabetic script (Clancier 2011 p. 764; Monerie and Clancier 2023 p. 70), so cuneiform records show no royal procedure.

**Across periods.** Ur III (3, contested) and Middle Assyrian (3) are the clearest cases; Postgate 2001 p. 193 compares them directly. Old Babylonian scores 2: Postgate 2001 p. 190 calls its practice a 'legal' mode, with parts of the institutional economy handed to private contractors.

### SCHOOL: Institutional scribal training

**Definition.** Institutional scribal training: from household apprenticeship to state-controlled training producing scribes for the whole polity.

**Why it matters.** Shared training is the obvious mechanism for shared tablet formats.

**Origin.** Seshat *Examination system* (poor fit); Veldhuis 2011; Robson 2001; Gesche 2001; Visicato 2000; the edubba debate.

| Score | Anchor | Operational test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | household apprenticeship, no shared curriculum | Household apprenticeship without a shared curriculum. |
| 1 | shared curriculum, household locus | A shared curriculum, taught in households. |
| 2 | institutional (palace/temple) schooling with shared curriculum | Institutional (palace or temple) schooling with a shared curriculum. |
| 3 | state-controlled training producing scribes for the whole polity | State-controlled training producing scribes for the whole polity. |

**Records that show it.** find contexts of school tablets; curriculum overlap across cities.

**Decision rules and boundary cases.**

- Old Babylonian scores 1: school tablets are usually found in the masters' houses (Charpin 2010 p. 22), and state control was lower than under Ur III (Boivin 2022 p. 612).
- Ur III is contested (1-3): Steinkeller 1987 p. 17 and Charpin 1995 p. 812 describe uniform state training, while Sallaberger 1999 p. 130 notes that practically no school tablets were found with the Ur III archives and that sons took up their fathers' profession.
- Old Assyrian scores 0: merchants learned to write at home (Larsen 2015 pp. 55-56).

**Across periods.** The scale separates curriculum from locus: Kassite exercise tablets are the same type across sites (Veldhuis 2000 p. 67), which supports a shared curriculum but not state control.

### LAW: Codified law and royal justice

**Definition.** Codified law and royal justice: from no evidence to a law collection together with royal judges and edicts.

**Why it matters.** Royal law can standardize legal formulae and thus legal documents, which bears directly on the Hammurabi test.

**Origin.** Seshat *Formal legal code*, *Judges*, *Courts*; Westbrook 2003; Charpin 2010; the debate on whether law collections were applied law.

### STD: State-imposed standards (measures, calendar, dating)

**Definition.** State-imposed standards of measure, calendar and dating.

**Why it matters.** Imposed standards are a direct form of legibility (Scott 1998); they show the state reaching into documentary practice.

**Origin.** Seshat *Calendar* (partial); Scott 1998; Brambor et al. 2020; Bartash 2019 on weight standards.

| Score | Anchor | Operational test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | local systems | Local systems only: each city or institution keeps its own measures and dating. |
| 1 | partial or local standard | A standard shared within one region or one institution (for example a palace measure beside a temple measure). |
| 2 | polity-wide standard measures or dating | One polity-wide standard in ONE domain: metrology OR dating and calendar. |
| 3 | polity-wide measures and dating/calendar imposed | Polity-wide standards in BOTH domains (metrology AND dating or calendar), demonstrably imposed by the crown, so that local variants disappear from the administrative record. |

**Records that show it.** royal measures (gur of Akkade, gur lugal, mina of the king), dating systems (year names, regnal years, eponyms, era).

**Decision rules and boundary cases.**

- Score imposition by the state, not mere use of common units.
- Sharper rule for 2 against 3 (8 Nov 2026, after ShG's note on Old Akkadian STD): a score of 3 needs evidence in both domains and evidence that local variants were displaced. A royal measure beside surviving local calendars is 2. Year names sent out by a chancellery count as dating only when they replace local formulae in the provinces.
- Applied: Old Akkadian 2 (measure of Agade, but 'the Akkadian Empire did not have a unified calendar', Foster 2016 ch. 6 §7); Neo-Assyrian 3 (eponym dating, the silver 'mina of Carchemish' after 717 and a court-run calendar: Baker 2023; Weeden 2023 §46.7; Steele 2011 p. 472); Middle Assyrian 2 to 2.5 (one official calendar and eponyms, but Ṭābetu keeps its own calendar, Postgate 2013 pp. 51-52); Hittite 2 at most (weights sealed by the queen, but letters undated, Hoffner 2009 p. 39).
- Old Akkadian is contested (1-3): Foster 2016 calls the measure of Agade the first attempt to impose a unified standard, but the governor of Umma kept local dating and the governor of Adab his own date formulae (Schrakamp 2020, PDF p. 549). Bartash 2019 p. 96 shows the reform was a process, not a single act.
- Ur III scores 3 (contested, 2-3): royal weights and a state calendar (Steinkeller 1987 p. 17; Bartash 2019 p. 110), while the provinces kept their own calendars (Sallaberger 1999 p. 234).

**Across periods.** Neo-Assyrian, Neo-Babylonian and Hellenistic score 3, Achaemenid 2; ED IIIa and IIIb score 1 for local systems.

**Contesting statements.**

- Old Akkadian, score 1: "The archive of the governor of Umma still used the local mu-iti dating system ... Meskigala, the governor of Adab, even used his own date formulae, which indicates a notable degree of independence, as the promulgation of year names was normally considered a royal prerogative." (Schrakamp 2020, OHANE I, ch. 9, §9.3 (PDF p. 549))
- Ur III, score 2: "In der Ur III-Zeit gebrauchten die einzelnen Provinzen weiterhin eigene Kalender, die sich aus den älteren Lokalkalendern herleiten. Diese Selbständigkeit im Kalender, die dann Isbi-Erra von Isin sofort beendet, ist übrigens ein gewichtiges Argument gegen die These vom Zentralismus des Ur III-Reiches." (Sallaberger 1999, p. 234)
