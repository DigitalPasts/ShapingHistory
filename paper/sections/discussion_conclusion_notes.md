# Material for the Discussion / Conclusions (from Section 5, round 4)

## Draft conclusion paragraph: what the VAE measures that the catalogue does not

The silhouette dataset we have released (92,876 CDLI tablets, each described by twelve VAE dimensions; Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.12787746) can be read as a set of diplomatic measurements. Measured against direct measurement of the photographed views of 47,714 well-preserved tablets, the twelve numbers recover three features reliably: the proportion of the obverse (R² = 0.82) and the thickness of the tablet against its width, at the sides (0.71) and at the top and bottom (0.70). Two more are recovered in part, enough to compare groups of tablets but not to describe a single one: how rectangular the obverse is (0.51) and how square its corners are (0.46). Features that depend on the exact line of the outline, such as roundness, taper or breaks, are not recovered (SI Section S6.4). Of these features, the CDLI catalogue records thickness for 15% of the same tablets and the shape of the corners for none. The case studies show that they carry history: square against rounded corners separate Puzriš-Dagan from Girsu receipts of identical proportion, and thickness separates the letters of Byblos from those of the other vassals of the Pharaoh. A notebook and a one-page guide released with the article return these measurements for every tablet in the dataset, with the reliability of each.

## Points from the former "Three scales of format" paragraph

- Format was set at three levels: the state (Ur III provincial forms converge in the last two reigns), the place of writing (Amarna letters resemble others made of the same clay; letters to the Pharaoh taller than letters at home), and the temple office (Eanna documents change in two steps, flocks first, storehouse later).
- Each is a historical claim that the tablets themselves can test, and each opens onto a larger corpus.
- Badreshany and Philip's call to join petrography with macroscale evidence of manufacture: tablet form is such evidence (Section 5, Amarna).
- Future pilot (not in the paper): clay colour from photographs under controlled light, as a non-destructive first guide to the place of making, to be tested against petrography.

## Closing point (ShG, 9 Nov 2026)

Shape alone already gives strong results with the data we have. Separating text type, scribe and workshop needs more detailed information recorded for the same tablets and run against the shape features: sign forms (palaeography), the scribe's hand, orthography, and clay characteristics (fabric, colour, inclusions). Supporting result (99b): within single archives, shape clusters follow text type first (Merkes A2 NMI 0.37, Ea-ilūtu-bāni 0.26, Murašû 0.18, Šamaš-hazir 0.18, Pedersén M 4 0.16) and hardly follow reign; across the ten Middle Assyrian archives at Aššur, distances between archive shapes follow their mix of text types (Mantel rho 0.52, p 0.003), not their museum.
