What changed against the earlier coding

Grounding the scores in the literature changed the cells below. The most consequential changes are in scribal training (Ur III and Old Akkadian move down, because OHANE authors place training in local or household settings), royal law (Neo-Babylonian and Achaemenid move up, on the evidence for royal judges and the "king's law"), and polity reach under the Old Babylonian kings.

Statistics on the 14 main periods

We asked one question: are the archival tablets of more centralized regimes more uniform in shape? No centralization index predicts more uniform tablets once the scores come from the literature. Polity reach and control of writing point the other way: the more centralized periods have slightly more varied tablets. None of these correlations is distinguishable from chance with 14 periods.

The one index that points toward uniformity is the institutional economy (palace and temple dominance, a small private sector). Its rank correlation with shape dispersion is −0.31 (permutation p = 0.28), and it stays negative in every one of 2,000 redraws of the uncertain scores. At the tablet level, one standard deviation on this index goes with tablets about 13% closer to their period's median shape (−0.025 on a mean absolute deviation of 0.19), but with only 14 periods the wild cluster bootstrap gives p = 0.15. In other words, the direction agrees with our earlier pilot result that uniformity follows the producing institution rather than the reach of the state, but 14 periods cannot establish it on their own.

Hammurabi: which break point?

We fixed five candidate dates from the literature before looking at the data and asked whether southern legal tablets (the former kingdom of Larsa) changed relative to northern ones (Babylon) at that date. "Most productive" was defined in advance as the break with the best fit (lowest AIC) for the shape comparison.

Methods text for the SI (draft)

Scoring the regimes from the literature

We scored each period on 12 parameters of centralization, grouped in three dimensions: polity reach (territorial reach, administrative hierarchy, fiscal extraction, communications), institutional economy (palace, temples, royal control of temples, and the private sector, which counts against centralization), and control of writing (standardized bureaucracy, scribal training, royal law, standard measures and calendar). Each parameter has four levels, 0 to 3, defined in advance (SI Table S_). We did not code the periods ourselves or ask experts to code them. Instead we collected statements from the current historical literature, mainly the chapters of the Oxford History of the Ancient Near East (2020 to 2023), with comparative studies and specialist works where those chapters were silent. For every statement we recorded the verbatim quotation, its exact place, the score it implies and its stance: it supports that score, qualifies it (accepts the institution but limits its reach), or contests it.

Scores follow from the statements by a fixed rule. The score of a cell is the median of the scores implied by its supporting statements, rounded down at a half. Its range runs over all statements. A cell is marked contested when a contesting statement implies a different score. In other words, when historians disagree, the disagreement is kept in the data rather than settled by us. Cells with no statement yet keep our earlier coding and are flagged.

Testing the hypothesis

The outcome is the dispersion of tablet shape within a period: the median absolute deviation of log(height/width) and the median distance of each tablet's 12-dimensional shape vector from the period's median vector. The unit of comparison is the period, and there are only 14 of them, so we used three tests that remain valid with few units. (1) A permutation test: we reassigned the index values at random to the periods 50,000 times; the p-value is the share of reassignments that give a correlation at least as strong as the observed one. (2) Score uncertainty: we redrew the whole score table 2,000 times, each cell from the spread of its quoted statements, and recomputed the correlation each time. (3) A tablet-level regression of each tablet's absolute deviation from its period median on the index, with museum fixed effects, and p-values from the wild cluster restricted bootstrap with Webb weights, which keeps the error rate near its nominal level with as few as 10 to 15 clusters.

The Hammurabi break points

For dated legal documents from 1830 to 1712 BCE we fitted, for each candidate year b, an ordinary least squares model of log(h/w) on region (south or north), phase (before or after b) and their interaction, with museum and damage as controls. The interaction is a difference-in-differences: how much more the southern tablets changed than the northern ones. The same model on each tablet's absolute deviation from its region-and-phase median measures change in uniformity. We compared the candidates by AIC and Akaike weights, and show the fit of every year in the window so the reader can judge whether the best candidate is a real optimum.
